Gear inches, in one paragraph

Gear inches ask: "if the pedals were bolted straight to the wheel, how big would that wheel be?" Chainring divided by cog, times wheel diameter. A 50x11 on a road bike is a 123-inch gear - one crank turn rolls you almost 10 meters, which is why it flies on the flat and stalls on a climb. A 34x28 is 33 inches: one turn, 2.7 meters, slow but unstoppable uphill. Development is the metric version of the same idea (meters per crank revolution), and speed is development times cadence. The overlap table above is the practical part: on a 2x setup several "different" gears repeat a ratio you already own, which is why riders cross-chain without noticing. If your easiest gear still isn't easy enough for your hills, the fix isn't fitness - it's a bigger cog or a smaller ring, and the range ratio tells you exactly how much you gain.
